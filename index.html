Two ways to run a group

The way most groups still work

Seven sets of books in seven places. A consolidation built by hand in a spreadsheet. Intercompany balances that do not agree until someone chases them. Management accounts that arrive six weeks after the month has closed, by which point the decision has already been made without them.

The audit happens once a year, on a sample of a few hundred entries out of hundreds of thousands. Whatever it finds is eleven months old.

The way it works with Elite Path

The same seven sets of books, untouched. Overnight, every entry across every entity is read, matched, eliminated and tested. By six in the morning the group is consolidated and each person opens the view that belongs to their level.

Not a sample — every transaction, every night, measured against UAE Corporate Tax, VAT, transfer pricing and IFRS. When something breaks a rule, the person who can fix it is told the same day.

And it does not stop at reporting. Alerts and notifications run against your own system and stay open until they are dealt with — what is still pending, what needs correcting, what is sitting on someone's approval. Each one goes to the person who can act on it, not into a monthly report nobody opens.

Internal Audit — Powered by AI

A traditional internal audit reads a sample. Ours reads all of it, overnight, and again the next night. Findings arrive while the payment can still be stopped and while the person involved still remembers the transaction.

  • Duplicate and near-duplicate payments across entities and banks

  • Round-sum journals, weekend postings, entries backdated into a closed period
  • Purchase orders raised after the invoice date
  • Credit notes issued to customers who never paid

Tax Management — Powered by AI

Tax problems in a group are almost never discovered by the tax return. They are built into ordinary transactions months earlier. We watch the transactions.

  • Corporate Tax position by entity, tracked through the year rather than at year end
  • Free zone qualifying income monitored, with a warning before mainland revenue puts the status at risk
  • Transfer pricing on every related-party charge, tested against arm's length
  • Transfer pricing disclosure and local file evidence assembled as you go
  • VAT treatment checked by supply: first residential sale, residential lease, commercial, mixed use
  • Input VAT apportionment and reverse charge on imported services
  • Economic substance and ultimate beneficial owner obligations tracked by entity

Built for real estate groups

A developer is never one company. It is eight, across two parent levels.

Land sits in one entity, the project is built in another, a third sells it, a fourth manages it afterwards and a fifth maintains it. Each one bills the others. On paper it is a group. In the accounts it is eight ledgers that only meet once a year, in a spreadsheet, under pressure. This is the group we built for.
